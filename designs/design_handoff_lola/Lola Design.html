01

The thinking

What I assumed, and the few decisions everything else hangs off.

The brief gave me almost everything; here's where I committed. Two contrast budgets, one system. Dad's surface uses only pure black-on-white and white-on-black — every pairing is 20:1, well past AAA — with 88pt minimum targets and 44pt+ type. Your setup surface relaxes to the reference's warm greys and normal density. They share one blue, one type family, one spacing scale, so it still feels like a single app.

I kept the system blue you picked (#1A73E8) for continuity with the live build, but warmed the neutrals and adopted the reference's signatures: the light-over-bold heading, rounded soft inputs, and the full-width CTA with a trailing arrow. The friendly-illustration warmth is carried by soft-tinted photo thumbnails and the calm, glowing voice states rather than literal mascots.

Voice is the interface

Every visual state maps to something Dad can hear happening: a pulse for listening, a slow spin for thinking, a waveform for speaking. The screen confirms; it never demands reading.

Calm, and self-stopping

Listening, thinking and speaking are quiet, centred states with no buttons to find — Lola simply stops on her own when she's done. Even errors stay calm: a soft amber heads-up, never an alarming red "you did something wrong" screen.

Setup is a tool, not a wizard

Fast list scanning, one-tap add, forgiving partial saves, encouraging photo progress. You can tag twenty objects without ever leaving a rhythm.

Dad can never fall into setup

There is no settings affordance anywhere inside the app. Setup lives entirely on the OS app icon (long-press → Configuración), so a low-vision user feeling around the screen can never land there.

02

UX review — your four questions

Concrete recommendations, not just options. Each is reflected in the prototype.
Question 01
The two-button home split — right call for a voice-first, low-vision user?

Keep two persistent buttons, but stop splitting them 50/50. For a low-vision user, predictable thumb geography is the whole game — "bright field on top = describe, dark field on bottom = ask" becomes muscle memory in a day. A single button with voice intent-routing sounds elegant but adds a speech-recognition failure point in front of the most common action (UC-1, "what's in front of me?"), and removes the reliable "just tap the top" path. That's the wrong trade for this user.

So: Describe dominates (~58%) and sits on top, because it's the most frequent action and needs zero speech to trigger. Ask is the smaller dark field below. Both are unmistakable color blocks, both AAA. I built all three (dominant / equal / single) into the prototype's Tweaks so you can feel the difference — but my recommendation is dominant.

Two buttons, rebalanced — Describe dominant, on top. Not a single voice button.
Question 02
Setup access via a 5-second logo hold — better pattern?

We landed on the cleanest answer: move setup out of the app entirely and onto the OS app icon. The caregiver long-presses the Lola icon on the phone's home screen and taps "Configuración" from the native menu (Android App Shortcut / iOS Quick Action). It opens Setup directly — even while the app is already running — so there's no timing race against the splash and, critically, no in-app surface for Dad to ever feel his way into.

  • No in-app door from the home screen — by design. A low-vision user feeling around the screen can never land in setup.
  • The splash carries a quiet legend — "Configuración: mantené presionado el ícono de Lola" — so the caregiver knows where to go without anyone having to explain it.
  • One config block in app.json (shortcuts on Android, UIApplicationShortcutItems on iOS). The prototype's "App icon → Setup" scene mocks the exact menu.
Setup lives on the OS app icon (long-press → Configuración). Splash legend points to it; zero in-app entry, zero risk for Dad.
Question 03
Objects vs Rooms — siblings, or should rooms contain objects?

Keep them flat siblings. Hierarchy feels tidy but fights all three use cases. Objects move — the thermos lives in the kitchen today and the bedroom tomorrow — so "where is X?" (UC-2) is answered from recent snapshots, not a static room→object tree. Forcing every object into a home room also adds a decision to every add, which kills the "tag twenty things fast" goal (UC-5).

You get the benefit of association without the rigidity: each object row shows a derived, non-editable "last seen in the Kitchen" hint, pulled from snapshots. Association as information, not as structure.

Flat tabs. Add a derived "last seen in {room}" hint on object rows.
Question 04
Anything else in IA, naming, or flow?
  • Drop the “internal name” (canonical slug) from the UI. The app still generates it under the hood as a stable key, but showing termo_de_papa to the caregiver only adds noise — it's not something they set or need to see.
  • No gear on Dad's home. The current build's gear is an accidental door into setup; with setup on the app icon, the home screen stays a pure two-button board.
  • Make the error states speak Dad's language — "No veo nada" spoken aloud, then back to the menu (no button); permission-denied is the only one that keeps a recovery action.
  • First-run should teach, not blank. Empty states name a concrete first action ("Add your first room") and explain why ("so Lola can tell him where he is").
  • The 5-photo cap is a feature, not a limit. Progress copy encourages at 1–2 ("add a couple more angles"), reassures at 3–4 ("looking good"), and closes cleanly at 5 ("that's plenty") — the add tile simply disappears, no error.

05

Edge cases — mocked in the prototype

Each is a scene in the prototype's left rail.
Long display names
Single-line truncation with ellipsis on list rows; full text wraps in the form. See "Object · edit (long name)".
Rooms 0 / 1 / 3 / 5 photos
Empty prompt → encouraging progress → clean stop at 5 (add tile disappears, no error).
Objects 0 / 1 / 20
Teaching empty state, single row, and a dense 20-item scroll that stays scannable.
Speaking at 2s vs 20s
Calm ambient glow + waveform holds indefinitely; nothing flickers or times out on long reads.
Camera / permission denied
Recoverable error in Dad's words with one large action — never a dead end.
Tap-to-cancel mid-speech
Full-screen stop target, green flash + "Listo", returns to idle. Try it on "Home · speaking".
